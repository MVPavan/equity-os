## Design decisions

Authoritative; tasks cite them by number.

### M1 Schema and seal (0016)

- `eqos.memory_items`: id; `entity_id` (FK `reporting_entities`, null only for the cross-stock
  kinds `SOURCE_QUIRK`, `OWNER_RULING`, `LESSON`); `kind` (the six §2 kinds); `statement` (1–500
  characters, the spec's "length-capped"); `epistemic_class` (the claims' five classes);
  `valid_from` date; `valid_to` date, open allowed, not before `valid_from`; `supersedes_item_id`
  (FK, earlier id); `recorded_at` and `created_xid` (trigger-set); `statement_search` (M6).
- `eqos.memory_supports`: id, `item_id`, and exactly one of `claim_id`, `version_id`,
  `definition_record_id`, `disposition_id` (`queue_dispositions`), `decision_id`,
  `capture_ref_id` (FKs; `num_nonnulls(...) = 1`), with
  `UNIQUE NULLS NOT DISTINCT (item_id, claim_id, version_id, definition_record_id,
  disposition_id, decision_id, capture_ref_id)`: no target is cited twice by one item.
- Triggers: item insert sets store time and xid, and refuses a supersession across stock or kind
  (`IS NOT DISTINCT FROM` on entity, equal kind). A support insert is refused unless its item was
  created in the same transaction. Every decision is a later transaction by another role, so this
  also refuses "a support added after a decision". A deferred constraint trigger refuses an item
  with no support at commit. Update, delete and truncate are refused on both tables
  (`eqos.reject_mutation`).
- `eqos.memory_sealed_digest(item)`: D1 encoding (`eqos.enc`) over the item row (generated column
  excluded), then the support count and each support's target columns in id order.
  `eqos.subject_digest` gains the `MEMORY_ITEM` branch (CREATE OR REPLACE, as 0014 did), so
  `ledger.store.subject_digest` and `append_decision` work unchanged.
- No stored content hash and no JSONB extras: the sealed digest is the item's content hash, and no
  acceptance needs extras (C3).
- Grants: SELECT to runtime, panel and owner; INSERT and sequence USAGE to runtime only. This
  matches the default row of `test_privileges_match_the_d2_matrix`, which stays unchanged.

### M2 Decisions, roles and invariant 11 (0016)

- `eqos.decisions` checks widen: subject `MEMORY_ITEM`; kinds `PROMOTE`, `REJECT_CANDIDATE`,
  `RETIRE`. `eqos.panel_reviews` subject widens to `MEMORY_ITEM`.
- `eqos.check_memory_decision()` (BEFORE INSERT, `WHEN subject_kind = 'MEMORY_ITEM'`): refuses
  unless the transaction is READ COMMITTED (M4); sets the decider from `eqos.decider_role()` and
  refuses anything but PANEL or OWNER; takes `eqos.lock_queue()`; checks the chain head (stale
  head refused, sequence set); requires a ruling from the owner. Legal: `PROMOTE` and
  `REJECT_CANDIDATE` on an empty chain by PANEL or OWNER; `RETIRE` by OWNER after `PROMOTE` when
  no promoted successor has retired the item. A `PROMOTE` of a superseding item also needs its
  predecessor `PROMOTED` (head `PROMOTE`, no other promoted successor). The subject must exist in
  `eqos.memory_items` (`decisions.subject_id` has no FK), and the supplied `sealed_digest` must be
  non-null and equal a non-null `memory_sealed_digest` (null-safe equality alone would accept a
  missing subject). `stamp_decision` sets time and chain digest unchanged.
- `eqos.check_panel_review` (CREATE OR REPLACE) maps a `MEMORY_ITEM` verdict `APPROVE` →
  `PROMOTE`, `REJECT` → `REJECT_CANDIDATE`; `REQUEST_CHANGES` has no memory decision.
- Python reuse: `SubjectKind.MEMORY_ITEM` and the three `DecisionKind`s in
  `contracts/ledger.py`; three `lifecycle.TRANSITIONS` entries; `append_decision` takes the queue
  lock first for `MEMORY_ITEM` and for a version `WITHDRAW`, as it does for proposals, and
  `lifecycle.validate` takes it before its subject lock (M4 lock order); `panel.review` gains the
  memory verdict map and a `_gate` branch (head empty; `eqos.memory_item_supported` true;
  predecessor promoted). `panel.review`'s `catalog`/`snapshots` become optional, required only
  for report versions, and it takes `describe: Callable[[Cursor, int], str] | None`, required for
  `MEMORY_ITEM`; `PgMemoryStore.decide` passes `retrieval.review_text`. `panel.py` imports nothing
  from `fundamentals.memory`.
- **Reviewer payload.** `retrieval.review_text(cursor, item_id)` is canonical JSON of the item
  row and its resolved supports from the M6 projection (`support_refs`), read at the lock time:
  per claim, quote, bounds or value, unit, qualifiers, anchor, capture blob SHA-256, its guidance
  contract and the M9 bound contract with its binding, and its version's stock, report period and
  kind, and status; per version, the same identity and status plus its narrative
  (`ledger.narrative`, as the version gate shows it) and its claims; per decision, kind,
  decider and ruling; per disposition, its row; per capture or definition record, its row. The
  gate's compared state is (digest, text), so a support whose status changes between the
  reviewer call and the lock makes the review stale.
- Retirement by supersession is derived, not written: the successor's one `PROMOTE` row retires
  the predecessor in that same transaction, which is atomic by construction.

### M3 Support predicate (0017)

`eqos.memory_support_holds(item_kind, support, as_of)` and `eqos.memory_item_supported(item,
as_of)`. Rows count only when `recorded_at <= as_of`; a null `as_of` means everything committed.

| Item kind | Allowed targets and their rule |
|---|---|
| `COMPANY_UNDERSTANDING` | version or claim; the (claim's) version passes V |
| `MANAGEMENT_TRACK_RECORD` | claims only: at least two (distinct by M1's unique constraint), at least one `SOURCE_QUOTE`, no two `SOURCE_QUOTE` claims at the same quote location (L below); each claim's version passes V. The outcome may be a quote (modified guidance) or a figure (met, missed) |
| `THESIS_AND_WARNING` | version passing V |
| `SOURCE_QUIRK` | definition record or capture (exists); disposition passing D |
| `OWNER_RULING` | decision by OWNER with a non-empty ruling |
| `LESSON` | version whose head is `REJECT` (exception R); disposition passing D; any decision |

- **R (rejected-version exception, recorded):** the spec's predicate requires a version support's
  latest validation to pass; a `LESSON` instead cites a rejected version as evidence that a
  mistake occurred, so it needs only head `REJECT` (and the version's own validations are not
  checked). `LESSON` cannot cite claims, so no rejected number becomes a support, and the
  version's `SupportRef` carries status `REJECTED`, never presenting its claims as trusted. A
  non-`LESSON` item citing that version fails V. Recorded as a spec note (orchestrator).
- **L (quote location):** `eqos.quote_location(capture_ref_id, anchor)` (0017, SQL) is the
  capture's `blob_sha256` plus the anchor's parsed integers
  `regexp_match(anchor, '^p(\d+)\.b(\d+)\.(\d+):(\d+)$')::int[]`, the coordinates the resolver
  reads (`citations.ANCHOR`). Two quote claims share a location when both are equal; an anchor
  that does not parse counts as shared (fail closed). Duplicate capture rows of one blob and
  equivalent spellings (`p030.b06.058:081`) therefore collide. M9 uses the same anchor parse.
- **P (a validation passes):** mode `AS_KNOWN`; `sealed_digest` equals
  `version_sealed_digest(v)`; a `MATCH` row for every claim of v (complete); no other state and
  no version-level row (`claim_id` null). `MATCH_UNTRUSTED`, which approval allows, fails P.
- **V (approved and trusted-valid):** head decision `APPROVE` or `IMPORTED_APPROVAL` and no
  descendant `APPROVE`/`IMPORTED_APPROVAL` (not superseded, not withdrawn); a latest **trusted**
  validation T exists (`creator` PANEL or OWNER, `AS_KNOWN`); and **every validation from T on**,
  of any creator, passes P. This is the spec's rule (the latest validation passes P) plus 0015's
  rule that a runtime check never makes a version eligible: a later `RETROSPECTIVE`, incomplete,
  non-`MATCH` or version-level validation fails V, and a runtime pass cannot rescue an earlier
  failure. An imported `APPROVED` version passes only once a trusted `AS_KNOWN` validation exists
  (gap G3). No spec change.
  "Latest" and "from T on" order validations by `recorded_at` (store time), then id within a
  transaction; identity values alone do not prove lock or commit order (Sol r2 note).
- **E (same stock):** when the item's `entity_id` is not null, every claim or version support's
  report has that `entity_id`. Null-entity items (`SOURCE_QUIRK`, `OWNER_RULING`, `LESSON`) are
  the explicit exception. E is part of `memory_support_holds`, so staging and `PROMOTE` share it.
- **D (unsuperseded disposition):** no later disposition on the same queue item.
- Staging: a second deferred constraint trigger (0017) refuses a candidate that fails
  `memory_item_supported`. A candidate citing a `HISTORICAL` version therefore fails V at commit.

### M4 The PROMOTE race (0017)

- **Commit-time re-check (own transaction).** The queue lock excludes other transactions, not
  later writes by the promoting transaction itself, which also share its cached store time. A
  deferred constraint trigger `memory_promotion_at_commit` (AFTER INSERT on `eqos.decisions`,
  `WHEN MEMORY_ITEM AND kind = 'PROMOTE'`, `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`) re-runs
  `memory_item_supported(subject_id, NULL)` at commit and refuses on failure; one trigger, reusing
  `memory_support_holds`.
- **PROMOTE is the last support-relevant write of its transaction (critical re-review F1).** A
  deferred trigger can be fired early (`SET CONSTRAINTS ... IMMEDIATE`), so it is not relied on
  alone. A new guard `eqos.refuse_after_promote()` raises when a memory `PROMOTE` decision with
  `created_xid = txid_current()` already exists (`decisions.created_xid` is the existing
  `bigint NOT NULL` set by `stamp_decision`, 0012/0014; `txid_current()` is the top-level id
  inside savepoints). It runs BEFORE INSERT, unconditionally, on every table whose rows can
  falsify a support: `eqos.validations`, `eqos.validation_claim_states`, `eqos.decisions` for
  `REPORT_VERSION` subjects, and every disposition table that carries `record_queue_time`
  (attached by the same loop 0006 uses). Unconditional is simpler and loses nothing: no
  acceptance needs a ledger or queue write after a promotion in one transaction. Invalidation
  before `PROMOTE` in the same transaction is caught by `PROMOTE`'s own BEFORE INSERT predicate;
  the deferred re-check stays as a backstop.
- **One lock.** `PROMOTE` holds `eqos.lock_queue()` from before its first store-clock read
  (`check_memory_decision`; `lock_queue` raises 55000 if the clock was read first, so the panel
  path takes it before its review row, as `panel.review` already does). Every write that can
  falsify a support holds the same lock from before its first store-clock read:
  - a disposition: `record_queue_time` (0009), unchanged;
  - a validation of any version (non-`MATCH`, version-level, incomplete, retrospective): new
    trigger `a_lock_support_writer` BEFORE INSERT on `eqos.validations` (sorts before
    `record_validation`) calls `lock_queue()`;
  - a `WITHDRAW`, and an `APPROVE` or `IMPORTED_APPROVAL` of a version with a predecessor (it
    supersedes every ancestor): the same function as a BEFORE INSERT trigger
    `a_lock_support_writer` on `eqos.decisions` (`WHEN subject_kind = 'REPORT_VERSION' AND kind IN
    ('APPROVE', 'IMPORTED_APPROVAL', 'WITHDRAW')`, sorts before `check_decision`), skipping
    approvals of root versions so root imports are untouched. An approval's own validation
    already read the clock, so a raw-SQL superseding approval without the lock is refused (55000).
    An imported successor's transaction reads the clock at version creation, so it takes the queue
    lock before `create_version`. The importer creates only root versions (one per report), so
    only the test helper `Ledger.imported` with a predecessor changes (Task 2).
- `eqos.lock_support_writer()` is about 15 lines; 0001–0015 are not edited and no D3 function is
  replaced. `check_memory_promotion()` (BEFORE INSERT, `WHEN MEMORY_ITEM AND kind = 'PROMOTE'`)
  only refuses unless `memory_item_supported(item, NULL)`; it takes no version locks (the queue
  lock already excludes every falsifier).
- **Lock order:** queue → one subject, on every path. Python takes the queue first in
  `approval_check`, `panel.review`, `append_decision` (proposals, memory items, `WITHDRAW`) and
  `validate`; the SQL trigger sorts before the subject lock, so a raw insert gets the same order.
  `PROMOTE` takes no subject lock besides its own item's. Subject-only lockers (`record_version`,
  content seals) never wait on the queue, since `lock_queue` after a clock read raises rather
  than waits. A raw transaction that locks a subject explicitly before such an insert can only
  deadlock; PostgreSQL aborts it, and nothing wrong commits.
- **Outcomes.** Writer first: `PROMOTE` waits, reads fresh at READ COMMITTED (O5) and refuses.
  `PROMOTE` first: the writer waits, stamps a later store time (monotonic store clock, 0004), and
  the item derives `NEEDS_REVALIDATION` from that time on. A cutoff between the two shows the
  state `PROMOTE` checked. Same transaction: `PROMOTE` then a falsifying write is refused at
  commit, so no committed promotion shares a store time with its own invalidation.
- **Why not SERIALIZABLE** (C1, amendment A1): SSI tracks only serializable transactions and
  every D3 writer runs at READ COMMITTED; a serializable `PROMOTE` also fixes its snapshot before
  it waits on the lock and would promote on stale state. `check_memory_decision` refuses a
  `PROMOTE` unless `current_setting('transaction_isolation') = 'read committed'`.

### M5 Status derivation

`memory.status.derive_status(head: DecisionKind | None, retired_by_successor: bool, supported:
bool) -> tuple[MemoryStatus, bool]`: none → `CANDIDATE`; `PROMOTE` → `PROMOTED`, or `RETIRED` if
retired by a successor; `REJECT_CANDIDATE` → `REJECTED`; `RETIRE` → `RETIRED`. The flag is
`NEEDS_REVALIDATION` only for `PROMOTED` and not `supported`. Retrieval reads the three inputs
from SQL at the cutoff. The triggers check only the heads they gate, as D3 does with
`_HEAD_STATUS`.

### M6 Retrieval, cutoff, flags, search

- `memory.retrieval.retrieve(cursor, *, query, stock, kinds, statuses, cutoff)`: items with
  `recorded_at <= cutoff` (all when null), the stock's `entity_id`, kinds when given, and
  `statement_search @@ websearch_to_tsquery('english', query)` when the query is non-empty. Per
  item it reads the head decision at the cutoff, a successor `PROMOTE` at the cutoff, and
  `memory_item_supported(id, cutoff)`; derives (M5); filters by `statuses` (default
  `{PROMOTED}`); orders by `ts_rank` desc, then id.
- **One snapshot.** `retrieve` and `provenance` run in one transaction opened
  `ISOLATION LEVEL REPEATABLE READ, READ ONLY`, so the item query and every `support_refs` query
  read one database state (at READ COMMITTED each query would see newer commits). Allowed: only
  memory decisions are restricted to READ COMMITTED (M4; widened from `PROMOTE` alone by the D4
  critical review 2026-10-15), and retrieval writes nothing.
- Each `RetrievedItem` carries status, flag and its supports as `SupportRef`s from
  `retrieval.support_refs(cursor, item_id, as_of)` (Task 2, shared with `review_text`). These are
  references, not a projection layer; status fields are derived at `as_of`:
  - claim: its version id, stock, report period, kind and version status; claim key; bounds or
    value, unit, qualifiers; guidance contract (quote claims); the claim's `Citation` (the
    existing contract: kind, `source_sha256` and every reference id — fact origin, interpretation
    and run, calculation trace, capture and anchor and quote, pair outcome, policy decision); and
    for a cited capture, its blob SHA-256;
  - version: identity and status, plus `claims`, one claim `SupportRef` per claim of the version
    (so a version support reaches figures and captures through its claims);
  - capture: id and blob SHA-256; definition record, decision, disposition: id and row.
  These are the "figures and captures" of §2 provenance; deeper trace operands stay reachable
  from the cited trace and interpretation ids through the existing D2/D3 tables.
- `memory.retrieval.provenance(cursor, item_id)`: the item and its supersession lineage (every
  predecessor and promoted successor, each with current status and its `SupportRef`s), so both
  stay visible (Stage 2 criteria).
- Valid-from and valid-to describe the statement's time and are not a cutoff filter (§2).
- M6 correction (D4 critical review 2026-10-15): stock None searches null-entity items.

### M7 `MemoryStore` interface (D-01)

  `UPDATE_FILES`, parsed by `ledger_contracts`, at the imported claim's key. Exactly one distinct
  contract binds; none or several bind nothing (fail closed). No model or drafter supplies it.
- `lifecycle.check_version`: a non-import version's quote claims take `bound_contract` (callers
  still may not pass `bound`; `_UNBINDABLE` unchanged); imports keep the importer's `bound`.
- The reviewer payload (M2) shows, per quote claim, the claim's contract and the bound contract
